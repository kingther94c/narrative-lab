## Question
For adding domain knowledge to an LLM, how do retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and fine-tuning compare, alone and combined, on open and commercial models?

## Data
Agricultural documents, not financial. USA: more than 23,000 PDFs and 50 million tokens across 44 states; the Washington subset used for evaluation is 573 files and about 2 million tokens. Brazil: the Embrapa "500 Questions 500 Answers" set. India: about 100,000 farmer queries, 2015 to 2022. The evaluation set is 273 human-curated Washington questions, each with a guideline describing a desirable answer.

## Method
- Pipeline: extract text from PDFs, generate questions, generate answers with RAG, fine-tune, evaluate with GPT-4.
- Not fine-tuned: Llama-2-chat 13B, Vicuna, GPT-4. Fine-tuned: Llama2 13B and GPT-4. Each is run with and without RAG, so GPT-4 gets a clean two-by-two.
- Fine-tuning targets were answers generated by Llama2-13B-chat with RAG over the same Washington documents the test questions come from. The number of training pairs is not stated.
- GPT-4 fine-tuning: LoRA, 4 epochs, batch size 256, learning rate 1e-4. Llama: 4 epochs, learning rate 2e-5, eight H100 GPUs.
- RAG: sentence-transformer embeddings in a FAISS index, top 3 passages. Retrieval was measured on its own: recall at k of 61.9% (k=1), 81.5% (k=3), 93.6% (k=10), falling to 75.0% at k=3 when the index grows from 573 to 3,888 documents.
- "Fully correct" was judged by GPT-4. Who or what scored the accuracy column is not stated.

## Outcome variable and horizon
Answer accuracy against the guideline, share of answers judged fully correct, succinctness on a 1 to 5 scale. No horizon.

## Result
Accuracy (the ± values are in the paper; their construction is not explained):

| Model | Fine-tuned | No RAG | With RAG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Llama-2-chat 13B | no | 76% ±2 | 75% ±2 |
| Vicuna | no | 72% ±2 | 79% ±2 |
| GPT-4 | no | 75% ±3 | 80% ±4 |
| Llama2 13B | yes | 68% ±3 | 77% ±2 |
| GPT-4 | yes | 81% ±5 | 86% ±2 |

Fully correct, without and with RAG: GPT-4 36% and 60%; fine-tuned GPT-4 45% and 61%; Llama-2-chat 32% and 49%; fine-tuned Llama2 29% and 49%.

- Empirical finding (abstract): fine-tuning added "over 6 p.p." and RAG a further 5 points, described as cumulative.
- My calculation, not the paper's: for GPT-4 the RAG gain is +5 points with or without fine-tuning, so the interaction is zero on the accuracy scale and +0.08 on the logit scale (0.29 against 0.37). On "fully correct" the RAG gain falls from +24 to +16 points, an interaction of -8 points and -0.33 logits. The sign of the interaction depends on the metric, and the stated ± of 2 to 5 points covers all of it.
- Fine-tuned Llama2 scored below the chat model without RAG (68% against 76%); the paper gives no explanation, and whether both started from the same checkpoint is not stated.
- Knowledge transfer: on questions similar across states, answers rated similar to the reference rose from 47% (GPT-4) to 72% (fine-tuned) and 74% (fine-tuned with RAG).
- No dollar costs; the conclusion says GPT-4's fine-tuning and inference costs "cannot be ignored".

## Look-ahead and memorisation
Not addressed. Public agricultural documents may be in pre-training data; no cutoff is discussed.

## What it settles for us
- Supports crossing fine-tuning with retrieval on the same model, and measuring retrieval separately: at recall of 81.5% about one item in five cannot be answered from the retrieved context, which is what our "correct passage supplied" level isolates.
- Does not address how to estimate interactions. "Cumulative" is read off four point estimates on one metric. This is the gap the logit-scale default fills, and the example above is a ready illustration of why the scale and the metric must be fixed in advance.
- Qualifies the roadmap's "commercial fine-tuning is absent": GPT-4 was fine-tuned here. The paper does not say how access was obtained, and the comparison is still across different base models.
- Shows the teacher confound in the wild: one roster model generated the fine-tuning targets, GPT-4 generated questions and judged answers, and GPT-4 was also under test. Our rule-based training labels and human gold avoid this.
- The setting is knowledge injection: training text and test questions come from the same documents. Our split is company-disjoint, so fine-tuning there teaches a skill, and the size of the fine-tuning effect here does not transfer.

## Limits
One domain; 273 test questions; an LLM judge with no reported human validation; unexplained intervals; no development set or tuning budget described; the paper names different generator models in different sections; no seeds.
