# Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning

- **Citation:** Tamera Lanham, Anna Chen, Ansh Radhakrishnan and 27 others, including Samuel R.
  Bowman and Ethan Perez (2023). arXiv 2307.13702, v1 of 17 July 2023 (the only version). No venue
  stated on the abstract page.
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702
- **Stream:** 12, forecast prompting (reasons and rationalisation)
- **Verified against source:** yes, 2026-11-22. arXiv HTML of v1 read as extracted text:
  abstract, sections 1 to 3.1, Tables 1 to 4. Figures read by caption; the addition-task section
  and appendices not read in full.

## Question
When a model reasons before answering, does the answer depend on the reasoning? Four tests
intervene on the reasoning and watch the answer.

## Data
Eight multiple-choice tasks: ARC Challenge, ARC Easy, AQuA, HellaSwag, LogiQA, MMLU, OpenBookQA,
TruthfulQA. Models: a 175-billion-parameter RLHF-tuned dialogue model, and a series of model
sizes (from Ganguli et al. 2023) for the scaling analysis.

## Method
- 100 reasoning samples per question (nucleus sampling, p = 0.95, temperature 0.8); the answer is
  read from next-token probabilities after "Given all of the above, what's the single, most likely
  answer?".
- Early answering: cut the reasoning after each sentence and ask for the answer. If the answer is
  already fixed before the reasoning ends, the reasoning is post-hoc.
- Adding mistakes: a pretrained model inserts a mistake into one step; the rest is regenerated.
- Filler tokens: replace the reasoning with "..." of the same length.
- Paraphrasing: a model that does not see the question rewords the start of the reasoning.
- Scaling: share of answers that change between no reasoning and full reasoning, by model size.

## Outcome variable and horizon
Share of answers unchanged after the intervention, summarised as an area over the curve (AOC);
accuracy with and without reasoning. Not a forecasting task.

## Result
Empirical findings.

- **Reliance on the reasoning varies by task (Table 2).** Early-answering AOC: AQuA 0.44, LogiQA
  0.26, MMLU 0.12, HellaSwag 0.12, TruthfulQA 0.11, OpenBookQA 0.07, ARC Challenge 0.05, ARC Easy
  0.02. On the three lowest tasks the reasoning changes the final answer less than 10% of the time;
  on AQuA more than 60%. Adding mistakes gives nearly the same ordering.
- **Reliance is not the same as benefit.** The accuracy gain from reasoning (AQuA +15.3, MMLU
  +3.8, HellaSwag -4.7, LogiQA +1.0 points) is weakly related to how much the answer depends on
  the reasoning. LogiQA relies on its reasoning yet gains little.
- **Not hidden computation, not hidden code.** Filler tokens give no accuracy gain; paraphrased
  reasoning keeps the accuracy of the original.
- **Inverse scaling.** For 7 of 8 tasks the 13-billion-parameter model changes its answer after
  reasoning more often than the 175-billion model; for most tasks faithfulness falls
  monotonically from 13B to 175B. The authors' reading (hypothesis): a model that can already
  answer confidently has less reason to use its reasoning.

## Look-ahead and memorisation

## What it settles for us
- **Post-hoc reasoning is the default for easy-to-answer items in strong models.** Where a
  frontier model already "knows" the answer, as it does for an old S2 episode whose outcome it
  remembers, the reasoning is likely written after the conclusion is fixed. That is what
  EXP-0029 found by other means.
- **Tests to borrow for S2.** Early answering (truncate the playbook reasoning and re-ask the
  probability) and adding mistakes (edit one cited fact and regenerate) measure whether the S2
  probability depends on the playbook output. EXP-0029 named "change or remove the top-weighted
  evidence" as its next step; this paper gives the protocol and the AOC summary.
- **Whether reasoning helps and whether it is faithful are separate questions.** The playbook
  arms need both an accuracy readout and a dependence readout.

## Limits
- One 2023 model family; multiple-choice tasks; answers read from token probabilities.
- Each test rules out one kind of unfaithfulness only; the authors say none is conclusive.
- The mistakes are generated by a model and are plausible "at least 80% of the time".
