# OpenThoughts: Data Recipes for Reasoning Models

- **Citation:** Guha, E., Marten, R., Keh, S., Raoof, N., Smyrnis, G., Bansal, H. and 44 others
  (2025). arXiv 2506.04178, preprint (no venue listed; the arXiv page carries an admin note on text
  overlap with arXiv 2505.23754 by other authors). Version read: v2 (5 June 2025), HTML, about 86%
  of the page (main text and appendices F-I in part).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04178 (HTML: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.04178v2)
- **Stream:** 14a (comparable domain: mathematics, code, science)
- **Verified against source:** yes, 2026-11-25, against the arXiv abstract page and the v2 HTML
  through an automated page reader. Only the first six authors were checked by name.

## Question

Which choices in building a supervised data set of teacher reasoning traces matter most for the
student: where questions come from, how many sources, how questions are filtered, how many answers
per question, whether answers are verified, and which teacher?

## Data

Questions from public math, code and science sources; answers (reasoning traces) generated by
candidate teachers. Final set OpenThoughts3-1.2M: 850K math, 250K code, 100K science. Traces
average about 11.6K tokens. Evaluation on eight to twelve benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025, AMC23,
MATH500, LiveCodeBench, CodeElo, CodeForces, GPQA Diamond, JEEBench and others), decontaminated by
indel similarity (75% threshold) and 13-gram overlap.

## Method

- Over 1,000 controlled runs. Each pipeline step was varied with the others fixed, at 31,600
  examples per run, student Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and the best option carried forward.
- Then the chosen recipe was scaled to 1.2M examples and trained into OpenThinker3-7B.
- Appendix: a second base model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) on 100K examples.

## Outcome variable and horizon

Benchmark accuracy averaged by domain. No horizon.

## Result

- **Empirical finding, teacher:** QwQ-32B produced better students than DeepSeek-R1, although R1 is
  the stronger model: code-domain average 44.2 against 42.3; math 44.2 against 41.6; science 53.2
  against 46.7; Phi-4-Reasoning-Plus far behind (29.0 / 30.6 / 38.2). The paper gives no mechanism
  beyond "better performance does not make a better teacher".
- **Empirical finding, answers per question:** sampling 16 answers per question is a way to grow a
  source at least 16-fold; fewer unique questions with more answers each did as well as or better
  than more questions with one answer (science: 36.2 with 16x against 35.5 with 1x).
- **Empirical finding, number of sources:** for code, the top 2 question sources averaged 41.3;
  top 1 39.9; top 4 38.6; top 8 37.0; top 16 36.4. The authors conclude source quality matters more
  than source diversity at this scale.
- **Empirical finding, question filtering:** keeping questions that elicit long answers (math,
  science) or that an LLM rates hard (code) beat random selection by about 4 (math) and 6 (code)
  points.
- **Empirical finding, answer verification:** no answer filter beat keeping all answers (math: all
  41.9; random filter 41.6; shortest 41.1; GPT verification 40.0; removing long paragraphs 38.0).
- **Empirical finding, scaling:** each improved step shifts the whole curve up from 316 to 31.6K
  examples; performance keeps improving to 1.2M on most benchmarks, with AMC23 and MATH500 starting
  to saturate. OpenThinker3-7B: AIME25 53.3, LiveCodeBench 51.7, GPQA-D 53.7, 12.4 points above
  DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on average over 12 tasks.
- **Empirical finding, trace length at 7B:** cutting traces to 0.3K tokens by removing
  self-reflection lowered average performance by 49.1%; dropping traces over 2,048 tokens lowered
  it by 33.3%.
- **Empirical finding, transfer:** training on math alone raised science scores; code-to-science
  gains vanished once in-domain science data was included.
- Base model: on 100K examples Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct ended higher than Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (AIME24
  54.3 against 37.0), though Llama gained more relative to its start.

## Look-ahead and memorisation

Benchmarks decontaminated (two checks, tuned to a 99.6% true-negative rate). No temporal
dimension.

## What it settles for us

- **Teacher choice is empirical, not "strongest available".** At 7B, a weaker 32B teacher beat
  DeepSeek-R1. Together with `li-2025-small-models-learnability-gap` (students <=1.5B learn better
  from a small teacher) and Xu et al. 2025 (stream 14a CSV), S1 should treat teacher choice as a
  factor to test on a small pilot, not a default.
- **"A lot of data" can come cheaply from many answers per question.** 16 samples per question was
  as good as 16 times more questions. For S1, where the number of distinct 8-K releases in the
  training window is fixed, sampling several teacher answers per item is the cheap volume lever
  (hypothesis for S1; untested below 7B).
- **Verification did not help here**, but every source here has hard, mostly correct teacher
  answers on math and code. S1's 4B teacher scores 82 on section R; whether keeping its wrong
  answers on label items hurts a 0.8B is open. S1 has rule gold, so filtering costs nothing and can
  be one arm.
- **Trace length points the other way from small-model studies.** Long traces helped at 7B, where
  `li-2025` found short traces better below 3B. For a 0.8B the short-trace default stands until
  tested (hypothesis).
- **Narrow sources beat many sources** at 31.6K examples for code. This is a caution against
  reading "diversity" as "more data sets"; what Zhang et al. 2024 and Harsha et al. 2025 show is
  diversity of *task and answer type*, which this paper did not vary (open question).
- No student below 7B, and nothing on financial or reading tasks.

## Limits

Students 7-8B; domains with verifiable answers; no RL; recipes chosen for average cross-domain
performance; preprint with a text-overlap admin note.
